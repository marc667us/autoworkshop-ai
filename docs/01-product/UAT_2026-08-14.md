## 🔴 The one thing that could not be done, and why

**There is no production path for an insurance sales pipeline or a marketing
campaign.** This is not a seeding shortcut; the feature does not exist.

Measured:

- `apps/api/src/crm/` contains **one controller**, `leads.controller.ts`,
  exposing `@Get()` and `@Patch(':id')`. There is **no POST** — nothing in the
  product creates a lead. They are written by the discovery agent.
- There is **no campaign entity** anywhere: no table, no module, no route, no
  screen. `grep -rl campaign apps/api/src` returns nothing.
- There is **no pipeline stage model**. `crm.leads.status` is a five-value
  CHECK (`new`, `qualified`, `contacted`, `converted`, `rejected`) on a single
  table — a lead status, not a sales pipeline.
- The insurance pack is **0 of 28 screens built**.

Seeding rows into `crm.leads` would have produced a "pipeline" the product
cannot create, display or advance — a fixture the product itself could never
have made. This repository has recorded four separate cases of exactly that,
and each one went green while being unreachable. So it is reported as a named
zero row on every verification run rather than faked.

**What building it would take**, so the decision is costed rather than vague:

1. A `crm` schema migration — `campaigns`, `campaign_members`,
   `pipeline_stages`, `opportunities`, all tenant-scoped with FORCE RLS.
2. Write endpoints — `POST /leads`, `POST /campaigns`, stage transitions with
   the same append-only audit discipline as approvals and payments.
3. Screens in the insurance pack, which currently has none.
4. A decision this plan has never taken: **is the insurance pipeline the
   platform selling insurance, or an insurer selling policies to workshop
   customers?** They are different products with different tenancy. That is an
   owner decision, not an implementation detail.

## What is real product behaviour and what is seeded

Stated per layer, because the distinction is the difference between a test that
means something and one that does not.

**Created by the real `SECURITY DEFINER` functions the application calls:**
`register_workshop` · `register_supplier` · `register_fleet` ·
`enrol_as_customer` · `provision_user_from_subject`.

**Seeded, in the same shape the domain service writes:** the five technician
memberships (the Staff and Roles screen is the product path and it works — it
needs an authenticated owner, and this realm has no password grant), and every
business record.

**The database was still the judge.** These tables carry real CHECK constraints
and BEFORE triggers, and they refused eleven separate mistakes before this
passed. Three worth recording:

- **`reject_unverified_publication`** — a workshop cannot list itself publicly
  before a platform administrator approves it. Administrator approval is now a
  modelled UAT step rather than something written around.
- **`reject_issued_line_change`** — invoice lines cannot be added after the
  invoice is issued. The invoice is created as a draft, lined, then issued and
  settled, which is the product's own order.
- **`parts.stock_items` has no quantity column.** Stock level is derived from
  the append-only `parts.stock_movements` ledger. `UAT-P001` opens at 19, ten
  jobs each draw one, and the availability view reads 9.

Those refusals are the reason the seeded records are worth something: the
workflow ordering is not asserted, it is enforced.
